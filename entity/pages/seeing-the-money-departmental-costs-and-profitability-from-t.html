Seeing the money: departmental costs and profitability from the accounts

Two dashboards now sit on top of the operating platform: the monthly management-accounts P&L (overview, department detail, 12-month forecast) and, since November 2026, a departmental cost and profitability dashboard built from Xero account transactions and the same accounts data. Access is directors-only. It is what turned "we think food cost is high" into a list of specific invoices to query.

What it found in its first week

  • A 100-litre keg invoiced at £603 against £267 for two 50-litre kegs of the same beer — £337 to query.
  • One craft keg bought with no discount at £81 a keg over the trade price.
  • Bottled soft drinks via the brewery at £30–32 a case against £20–23 elsewhere.
  • Three drinks wholesalers overlapping on spirits and mixers.
  • Food spend +63% in the month supply switched wholesaler, with invoices keyed as summaries so unit prices cannot be compared.
  • A possible duplicate posting of £308 under two suppliers on the same day.
  • Accounts charging £26,541 less F&B cost of sales than purchased, March–September — no month-end stock counts.

Why this belongs in the JV story

None of this needed AI. It needed the data in one place and someone to look. The AI layer's job is to look every day, across every supplier, at every hotel — and to connect the finding to the fix: a stock count in HosF&B, a price check on the next invoice, a supplier switch costed.

How it joins up

The P&L dashboard, the costs dashboard, HosF&B and HosTIME are being wired into one chain: Xero bills → last price per ingredient → dish cost → menu P&L; till sales + clocked hours → daily P&L; stock takes → true consumption → the accounts. The management accounts stop being a monthly surprise and become the sum of what the operating platform already knows.
