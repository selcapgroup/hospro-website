    1 · The opportunity

    Independent hotels are desperate for three things

    Talk to any owner or general manager of an independent hotel and the same three problems come up within five minutes: maintenance that costs too much and takes too long, room rates set blind against the competition, and not enough business through the door — especially meetings, weddings and events. The 2026 industry buyer's guide for hotel management systems confirms the backdrop: systems "buckling under rising acquisition costs, OTA dependence and siloed data", and a market shifting from feature checklists to platforms that act as an interaction layer across operations, revenue, marketing and guests.

    Figure A · The three pains every independent hotel names — and the HosPRO module that answers each

    The market

    Industry guidance segments the market into large hotels (100–500+ rooms, departmental silos), boutiques and independents (20–100 rooms, high-touch) and B&Bs and small hotels. HosPRO is built for the middle: properties too complex for a diary and too small for an enterprise suite. In the UK alone that is thousands of country-house hotels, inns, spa hotels and wedding venues — and the pattern repeats across Europe and the Gulf.

    What buyers now say they want

    The buyer's guide lists role-based permissions and audit trails, deep departmental integrations, unified guest profiles, fast onboarding (three weeks for a small property) and — above all — interoperability over isolated features. Independents add: cutting OTA cost, keeping staff agile and avoiding training overhead. Every one of those is a HosPRO design principle already.

    Where the incumbents stop

    PMS-centred suites (Cloudbeds, Mews, Little Hotelier, RoomRaccoon) run reservations, channels and billing, and are now adding AI on top of booking data. None of them run the back of house: who fixed the shower, how long the room took, whether the fridge was in range, who is on site. That operational layer — and the data it generates — is the gap HosPRO fills and the asset a JV builds on.

    3 · The problem

    How an independent hotel actually runs today

    A 120-room hotel with a spa, restaurant and events runs on a patchwork: a property-management system for rooms, a printout for housekeeping, WhatsApp for maintenance, a clipboard for fire checks, spreadsheets for F&B figures, email for sales enquiries and a monthly accountant's pack for the numbers. None of them talk to each other. The glue is people — and the glue breaks every day.

    Figure 1 · Six systems, zero connections — the daily reality in most independent hotels

    Why the big suites don't fix it

    Enterprise hotel software is priced and designed for chains: long implementations, per-module licences, and workflows the hotel must bend to. Independents end up using 20% of it and still run WhatsApp on the side.

    Why point apps don't fix it

    A maintenance app here, a housekeeping app there — each with its own login, its own data and no idea the other exists. The hand-off between departments is exactly where the value is, and exactly what point apps miss.

    What the opportunity is

    One connected operating layer, built around how a hotel actually works, cheap enough for a 70-room property, installable on any phone in a day — and generating the operational data that makes AI genuinely useful.

    4 · Our approach

    Common-sense systems: the six principles

    HosPRO was not designed in a workshop. It was built week by week inside Brandon Hall Hotel & Spa — a 120-bedroom country-house hotel with a spa, restaurant, meetings and weddings — by the people running it. Every principle below is a lesson paid for on the floor.

    Figure 2 · The six operating principles every HosPRO module is tested against

    What "common sense" means in practice

    • If a feature takes more taps than WhatsApp, it loses — so it is redesigned until it wins.
    • Data is captured as a by-product of the work (a completion photo), never as a separate task ("fill in the log").
    • The desktop is for managers and reports; the floor runs on phones.
    • Thresholds, defaults and assignments are configured per hotel in minutes, not coded per client.
    • Each hotel keeps its own data in its own collections; the platform is shared.

    Where this comes from

    HosPRO is run by hotel operators and commercial leaders, not a software vendor. The same team manages Brandon Hall's commercial plan, its P&L, its fire audit preparation and its brand — so the software is shaped by owning the outcome, not by selling licences.

    That is the asset we bring to a JV: the domain, the live customers, the data model and the operating know-how. What we want from a partner is the engineering platform, AI and agent capability to turn it into a product at scale.

    5 · The platform today

    One app, one database, every department

    HosPRO Mobile is a single installable web app. A person picks their department, their name and a four-digit PIN and sees only their tiles. Managers land on a live hotel overview. Everything writes to one shared database, so the desktop portal, the phone app and the reports always agree.

    Figure 3 · The HosPRO hub — the PMS feeds it, ten modules run on it, two outputs summarise it

    Who uses it

    Directors and GM · front office · housekeeping (lead + housekeepers) · maintenance (manager + fixers) · F&B · sales & events · fire marshals. Each has one PIN and one icon on their phone.

    How it is installed

    Open the link on a phone → Add to Home Screen. No app store, no device management, no per-seat licence. A new department link is live in under a minute.

    What it runs on

    Vanilla web (PWA) on GitHub Pages, Google Firebase (Firestore, Cloud Functions) and a small Google Cloud VM for the guest Wi-Fi relay. Total hosting cost for a 120-room hotel: tens of pounds a month.

    6 · How it connects

    Information flow: from a tap on a phone to the owner's report

    Every piece of information in HosPRO is captured once, at the point of work, and then flows upward through five layers without being re-typed. The diagram traces a single day's data: where it is born, where it is stored, what is derived from it, and who sees it.

    Figure J · Five layers of information flow — captured once, derived many times, seen by the right person

    Up: capture to insight

    Data moves up through the layers automatically. A photo taken in Room 61 at 09:40 is, by 09:41, a job, a room flag, a KPI input and a line on the needs-attention list — with no human copying anything.

    Down: decisions to the floor

    Decisions flow back down the same path: a manager's approval, a redeployment, a rate change or an allocation lands on the right phone as a push and a badge, with the context attached.

    Sideways: between departments

    Because every event is keyed on room, person and time, any module can read any other's events. That is how reception sees housekeeping status, HosREV sees rooms out of order, and F&B sees tonight's event covers.

    Practices we hold to

    • Add, never break. New modules sit beside working ones; old apps stay as fallbacks until the new one is proven (HosFIX standalone is still there behind the one-app version).
    • Change files, not folders. Only the files that changed are uploaded; two people never deploy from different local copies (we learned this the hard way on 1 November).
    • Operational data is immutable. Corrections are new records; nothing is deleted in `hosfix_jobs` (rules block deletes).
    • Approvals and thresholds live in the data (£50 gate, approvers, default fixers), not in code, so each hotel configures its own.
    • Photos compressed on the phone; large media kept out of the job document (`hosfix_photos`) so sync stays fast on hotel Wi-Fi.
    • Every change is render-tested (syntax checks and a headless browser run for each role) before it is uploaded.
    • Clean-start cut-offs when a module is rebuilt, so history is not polluted by test data.

    Honest limits

    No real authentication yet (PINs, open rules on shared collections). No automated test suite beyond render checks. No CI/CD, no staging environment, no monitoring beyond Firebase's console. Single Firebase project shared by two hotels. These are exactly the items a partner platform solves first.

    10 · Database design

    Lifecycles: how a job and a room change state

    Two state machines carry most of the operational logic. They are deliberately small — a fixer never has to choose from twelve statuses — and every transition writes who, when and (where relevant) a photo.

    Maintenance job (hosfix_jobs.status)

    Default assignees are set per hotel; the approval threshold and approvers are configuration, not code. Completion writes maintStatus: complete onto the room record so housekeeping sees "✓ Completed by maintenance".

    Room for the day (hoshouse_rooms.status)

    Allocation happens before pending: the lead chooses auto (skill, rating, floor) or manual. Each transition stores start/finish timestamps, so room duration and rooms-per-hour are measured, not estimated.

    Access model

    Tiers

    Manager (admin) — everything, approvals, reassign, reports. Lead — their department, can close incidents. Fixer — only jobs assigned to them. Staff — their department's modules and their own reports.

    Department → modules

    Housekeeping: HosHOUSE · Maintenance · Rota. Maintenance: Maintenance · Fire · Compliance · Rota. Reception: Reception · Housekeeping board · Maintenance · Fire · Rota. F&B: F&B · Maintenance · Fire · Rota. Sales: Sales · Fire · Rota.

    Hardening (next)

    Today: app-level PIN login with open rules on shared collections, matching how the hotel operates. Next: Firebase Auth with role claims, per-collection rules, audit log — a natural fit for a partner platform with permissions and approvals built in.
